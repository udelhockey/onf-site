Logo

Two marks, two jobs

ONF has two logos. The skater mark is the everyday workhorse — it works tiny, in one color, anywhere. The roundel badge is the full crest — it carries the name, tagline, wave, and net, so it needs room. Use the badge where people stop and look; use the mark everywhere else.

Primary · Skater mark

Header, favicon, social avatars, watermarks, buttons, email signatures, player cards. One flat color only. Vector (SVG) from the original Illustrator file, so it stays sharp at any size.

Ink — default on white & light.
White — on navy & dark sections.

Secondary · Roundel badge

Use for: home hero, about page, event posters and programs, jerseys and merch, social cover images, sponsor decks.

Minimum size: 240px wide on screen, 2 in. in print. Below that the tagline ("Putting a check on childhood diseases") becomes unreadable — switch to the skater mark.

Backgrounds: white or navy only. It's already full-color, so never place it on ice blue, logo blue, or a busy photo.

Don't crop it, pull the skaters out of the circle, or recolor the wave.

Do

Keep clear space around the skater mark equal to the height of the skater's helmet.

Use a lockup when space allows — the mark alone doesn't say "Open Net Foundation" to someone new.

Use the SVG files anywhere on the site; keep the EPS as the print master.

Don't

Use the badge as a header logo or favicon — it's too detailed.

Add gradients, outlines, shadows, or a second color to the skater mark.

Stretch, rotate, or flip any mark, or retype the wordmark in another font.

Open item — which blue? The logo's blue is specified in the original artwork as C70 M15 Y0 K0, which is #29ABE2 on screen. The site's Ice Blue is #6EC1E4 — lighter and grayer, and it reads as a different brand next to the badge. Recommendation: adopt #29ABE2 as the brand blue everywhere. It still needs dark text on top (it fails contrast with white), so the button rule doesn't change.
Source files: the Illustrator EPS (Sept 2022) is the master for print. It also holds an event version of the badge with "Face off for teenage mental health" set in the net — the same slot can carry each event's name for posters and social graphics. Text in the artwork is converted to outlines, so no font is needed to reproduce the wordmark.
Voice & tone

How we write

Do

Write in sentence case. "Past champions," not "Past Champions."

Lead with impact and people — dollars raised, kids helped, causes named.

Sound like a teammate: warm, plain, grateful.

Name the beneficiaries (NAMI, Autism Delaware, Ronald McDonald House).

Don't

Title Case Every Word Of A Sentence (it currently does this).

Lean on jargon like "leverage synergies" or nonprofit boilerplate.

Set long paragraphs in ALL CAPS or center-align body copy.

Bury the cause under event logistics.
